**Update — 5 October 2026:** The current [emidm documentation](https://ojwatson.github.io/emidm/) describes Python research software for differentiable epidemiological modelling. The Nature article linked below is a Perspective on the wider field; it does not describe or validate emidm. See the updated [project page](/project/emidm/) for its present scope.

I recently contributed to **Nature (2025)** on artificial intelligence for infectious disease epidemic modelling. The paper reviews how modern AI methods can support epidemic forecasting, surveillance, intervention planning, and model acceleration while still needing careful public-health interpretation.

- Paper: [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08564-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08564-w)
- Related tool: [emidm](/project/emidm/)
- Related research section: [Methods that connect the work](/research/#methods-that-connect-the-work)

Alongside this, my group continues work on emulator-based approaches that improve calibration speed and scenario exploration for policy-facing analyses. The aim is practical: make complex infectious disease models easier to interrogate under uncertainty, without losing sight of the assumptions that make those models useful.
